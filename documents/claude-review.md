# Critical Issues (Would likely pass on this candidate)

1. The About page actively undermines your seniority
   "The interest came and went rather quickly as I wasn't sure if I could break into this industry with my limited skills" — this is the first thing I read about your background after your photo. For a senior role, this reads as deeply unconfident. You've shipped real products. Rewrite this from a place of authority, not accident. Similarly, "chanced upon UX Design" signals an unintentional career path. Senior designers own their narrative.

2. Half the portfolio is bootcamp/conceptual work
   The Grab redesign was done under a mentor ("my mentor, Daniel See"). The flower delivery app is a concept. The cycling tour redesign is a personal passion exercise. That's 3 of your 6 projects that a hiring manager would immediately discount. For a senior role, I expect 4–5 shipped products with real users and real stakes. These conceptual pieces push your actual professional work to the background.

3. No measurable outcomes on most projects
   The BM Auto-Scheduler has a clear outcome (63% time reduction, 40 min → previously 120 min — strong). But the OmniHR form only says "projected to reduce completion time by 25%" — projected by whom, validated how? The cycling tour, Grab, and flower delivery projects have zero impact metrics. Senior designers are expected to tie design decisions to business outcomes.

4. "Work In Progress" as your first listed project
   OmniHR Performance is shown first in the grid with a "Work in Progress" stamp. I understand wanting to show recent work, but leading with an incomplete project signals poor editorial judgment about your own portfolio. Either finish it or move it to the bottom.

# Significant Gaps (Would ask hard questions in interview)

1. No formal research is mentioned in your most recent case study:
   1. In the OmniHR Add Employee Form write-up, you explicitly state: "We did not conduct any formal research." For a senior designer, this is a flag. You relied on CS team feedback and founder instincts — that's fine in practice, but the way it's framed makes it sound like you accepted that limitation rather than advocating for even lightweight research methods like a quick Jobs-to-be-Done session or task analysis.

2. No evidence of design systems, component ownership, or design-to-dev handoff
   1. Across all case studies, there's no mention of maintaining a design system, contributing to a component library, writing specs, or collaborating on tokens. For complex B2B SaaS at a senior level, this is table stakes. Even a single mention of "I own and maintain our Figma component library" would help enormously.

3. No cross-functional leadership or conflict resolution stories
   1. Senior designers are expected to navigate stakeholder disagreements, push back on scope creep, and align PMs and engineers. The BM case study shows good collaboration but frames you as executing a vision. The OmniHR case study mentions scope was deprioritized — but there's no insight into how you managed those trade-offs or influenced decisions.

4. Your portfolio coverage jumps from 2021 to 2023 with nothing more recent
   1. The most recent case study is from 2023. We're in 2026. Where are the last two years of work? Even if the work is under NDA, there should be a brief placeholder with context ("I've been leading design for X feature at OmniHR — reach out to discuss").
